# Tray panel opens in full view on Windows (#64) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans to implement this
> plan. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking. **Two tasks, so one commit per task on
> branch `issue-64`.** Two stops: Final Step 3 (owner flies the VM) and Final Step 4 (gate 2). Run
> every launch and stop yourself. Do not hand one to a sub-subagent.

**Goal:** On Windows, either tray click opens the whole panel next to the taskbar, a second click
or a click elsewhere closes it, its rows work, and a flight renders above it, with Mac behavior
unchanged.

**Architecture:** A pure function works out the panel's rectangle from the taskbar's edge, and both
opening and every height change use it, so the panel stays anchored to the taskbar. The solid look
needs one window option and one added font name, with no platform check in the page. Clicks and
stacking are fixed from what the VM shows: Task 2 goes red first, and code lands only for what is
red.

**Tech Stack:** Electron 43.2.0 (`screen`, `Tray`, `BrowserWindow`), the VM commands in
`docs/windows-vm.md`, a throwaway PowerShell helper that stays out of the repo.

**Spec:** GitHub issue #64 (`gh issue view 64`). Decisions and fog: parent #60. No design spec.

## Decisions to check at gate 1

1. **How the second tray click closes the panel is picked by a measurement, not now.** Electron
   43.2.0 fires tray `click` on `WM_LBUTTONDOWN` (`notify_icon_host.cc:295-312`), and the taskbar
   takes focus from the same press, so blur may come first and the click would reopen the panel.
   The source cannot say which comes first. Task 2 Step 2 measures the gap, then:
   - No reopen in 10 tries: no guard code.
   - Reopens, and the gap does not grow with a longer press: ignore a tray click that comes less than
     `POPOVER_BLUR_CLICK_MS` after a blur. The value is 3 times the largest measured gap and at least
     100 ms, and the comment labels it measured plus a margin guess. Reason to prefer this: it has no
     stale state, and it does not depend on tray bounds, which may not point at the icon when the
     icon sits behind the `^` arrow.
   - Reopens, and the gap grows with a longer press: a time window cannot work. The blur handler sets
     a flag when the cursor is inside `tray.getBounds()`, and the next tray click clears the flag and
     does nothing. The cost is that a focus change with the pointer resting on the icon eats one later
     click.
2. **The panel centers on the icon, not left-aligned as on the Mac.** That is where Windows' own tray
   flyouts sit. It clamps to the work area, so near the screen corner it pulls in. The alternative,
   the Mac rule (left edge at the icon), would leave the panel off-center and clamped on most PCs.
3. **Top and side taskbars get the same function, and only arithmetic proves them.** Windows 11 offers
   only a bottom taskbar, and the VM is Windows 11 (build 26300, bottom, read from `StuckRects3` at
   planning). Windows 10 still allows all four edges, and nobody knows the coworker's version. So:
   - **Handled:** the panel sits inside the work area, `POPOVER_GAP` from the taskbar's edge, and the
     edge is read from where the work area is smaller than the display. Auto-hide reserves nothing, so
     that case uses the display edge nearest the icon.
   - **Tested:** every edge plus auto-hide by a throwaway script against the real function (Task 1
     Step 3). One try at a real top taskbar on the VM with the registry (Task 1 Step 8), which may
     not work on this build, and the report says what happened. Nothing else. A side taskbar is
     never tested live unless that try works.
4. **Solid background through the window's `backgroundColor: '#1e1e20'`, Windows only, and no page
   CSS.** `#1e1e20` is the RGB of the panel's own `rgba(30,30,32,.72)`, so no color is invented, and
   the panel on top composites to the same color. Corners may show the CSS radius's hairline arc
   inside a square window. That is decided by looking (Task 1 Step 6), with a one-line fix ready.
5. **VM-only patches prove three things that nothing else can reach there.** The VM has no Jira token,
   so the ring is empty and Replay has no row. On a 1024x768 screen the plane's line sits above the
   panel, so "above" cannot be seen. The patches are never committed, and Final Step 1 proves that.
6. **No variant round.** The ticket says "Same design and no 3-variant round, per #60", so the
   `# Visual rule` does not apply.

## What was checked at planning

Read, not run. No app was launched at planning.

- **Tray events:** `click` fires on press and `right-click` on `WM_RBUTTONDOWN` or `WM_CONTEXTMENU`
  (`notify_icon_host.cc:295-312`). Electron never sets a notify icon version, and from memory of the
  Windows docs that means no `WM_CONTEXTMENU` on a mouse click. If both arrive, one right click would
  open and close the panel. Task 2 Step 2 shows it.
- **Double click:** a fast second left press arrives as `double-click`, not `click`
  (`notify_icon.cc:76-82`), and `setIgnoreDoubleClickEvents` does nothing on Windows (#62's plan). So
  today a fast second click does nothing.
- **Levels:** on Windows, `setAlwaysOnTop`'s level name only chooses whether the window sits behind
  the taskbar, and neither `pop-up-menu` nor `screen-saver` is on that list
  (`native_window_views.cc:1174-1200`). So the Mac's "panel below overlays" rule does not hold, and
  from memory of Chromium, the window raised last wins. A panel opened mid-flight should land above the
  plane. Task 2 Step 3 goes red on it. A new `setAlwaysOnTop` call raises through `SetWindowPos` with no
  activation, so it should not blur the panel.
- **Focus:** `show()` calls `widget()->Activate()` (`native_window_views.cc:592-598`). Whether Windows
  lets that take the foreground after a tray press decides whether blur ever fires, so a click
  elsewhere proves it (Task 2 Step 2).
- **Sizing:** `setBounds` updates the size limits of a non-resizable window
  (`native_window_views.cc:887-903`), so it works with `resizable: false`.
- **The VM:** Windows 11 Pro Arm64 10.0.26300, no `electron` running. `WorkingArea` read over SSH
  equals the bounds, because SSH runs in session 0, so read it only from the desktop session.

- [ ] **Step 3: Write the function and prove it by arithmetic**

`windowsPopoverBounds` goes in `main.js` above `togglePopover`. It takes `display` as
`{ bounds, workArea }` and touches no Electron object, so a script can run it.

The edge is the first of bottom, top, left, right where the work area stops short of the display. If
none does, the edge is the display edge nearest the icon's center.

| Edge | Main axis | Cross axis |
|---|---|---|
| bottom | `y = min(workArea bottom, tray top) - height - POPOVER_GAP` | `x` centered on the icon |
| top | `y = max(workArea top, tray bottom) + POPOVER_GAP` | `x` centered on the icon |
| left | `x = max(workArea left, tray right) + POPOVER_GAP` | `y` centered on the icon |
| right | `x = min(workArea right, tray left) - width - POPOVER_GAP` | `y` centered on the icon |

Then clamp the result to the work area and `Math.round` it. The `min` and `max` with the tray's own
edge cover auto-hide and the flyout, where the icon sits inside the work area.

`check-bounds.js` (scratchpad) cuts the function's text out of `main.js`, evaluates it, and runs fake
displays and tray rectangles. Cases: each of the four edges at 1024x768 and at 1920x1080, each edge with
auto-hide, a display at a negative `x`, and the icon close to the corner. Assert for each: the panel is
fully inside the display, no pixel overlaps the taskbar's rectangle, and it is `POPOVER_GAP` from
the taskbar. For a bottom taskbar, assert that the bottom edge is the same at heights 232, 247, 328, and
344. Run with `node`. Expected: every case passes. Report the raw count.

## Tray panel opens in full view on Windows — #64 (2026-11-05) — ACCEPTED & CLOSED; commits 43abba6, 87b7fe0, 6436c7f

Either tray click now opens the whole panel next to the Windows taskbar, a second click or a click
elsewhere closes it, and a flight renders above it. A pure function works out the panel's rectangle
from the taskbar's edge, so opening and every height change anchor to the taskbar and the bottom edge
stays put as rows are added. The panel centers on the icon, like Windows' own tray flyouts, and has a
solid `#1e1e20` window background and Segoe UI. The Mac runs none of this.

The plan was wrong about the tray: `click` and `right-click` fire at release, not on press. A press
blurs the panel within about 20 ms, and the click arrives at the end of the hold (808-899 ms after blur
on an 800 ms hold), so a time window cannot work. The blur handler sets a flag when the panel is visible
and the cursor is inside the tray's bounds, and the next tray event clears it and does nothing. The
visible check is there because a hidden panel blurs too: the first version ate the tray click after the
Test flight row. A fast second click arrives as `double-click` with no `click` after it, so that event
also goes to the toggle, or it would leave the flag set.

Stacking: on Windows the overlay and panel levels share one topmost band, and the last raised wins. A
panel opened mid-flight covered the plane until `togglePopover` re-raised the overlays last. A flight that
starts while the panel is open needed no code.

### STATE.md gotchas to add

- On Windows, `setAlwaysOnTop` level names do not order windows: overlays and the panel share one topmost
  band, and the last raised wins (#64).
- On Windows, tray `click` and `right-click` fire at release, after the press has already blurred the panel
  (measured, #64). A hidden panel also blurs. A fast second click is `double-click`, with no `click`.
- On the Windows VM, `Date.now()` in a running Electron can lag the system clock by about a minute, so do
  not compare its timestamps with another process's. Host load can stall the VM for seconds (#64).
