# Plane Above Every App (#12) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans.
> Single-task plan — the change stays uncommitted in the working tree for gate 2 (AGENTS.md).

**Goal:** The overlay renders above every application window — including the reported failing
case, a plain regular app window covering the plane — and above the menu bar and Dock.

**Architecture:** The overlay already requests the right level: `'screen-saver'`
(NSWindow level 1000, above regular apps at 0, Dock ~20, menu bar ~24). Since a *regular*
window covered the plane, the level is not sticking. Prime suspect: on macOS,
`setVisibleOnAllWorkspaces` can reset the window level (long-standing Electron behavior),
and `main.js` calls it *after* `setAlwaysOnTop`. Fix: assert the level after every point
that can re-stack the window — after `setVisibleOnAllWorkspaces`, after `showInactive()`,
and after the AeroSpace `move-node-to-monitor` release. No new level is needed:
`'screen-saver'` is Electron's highest named level and already satisfies the owner's
"over menu bar + Dock too" decision — once it sticks.

**Tech Stack:** Electron `BrowserWindow` window levels, existing `releaseFromTilingWM()`
AeroSpace escape hatch. All changes in `main.js`; `plane.html` untouched.

## Known risks

1. **Root cause is hypothesized, not proven.** The level-reset-by-`setVisibleOnAllWorkspaces`
   story fits "a regular window covered it," but if the real culprit is something else
   (e.g. AeroSpace tiling the overlay before `releaseFromTilingWM` lands, or a one-off),
   the re-assertions may not fix the owner's sighting. Step 1's repro attempt is what keeps
   this honest.
2. **`isAlwaysOnTop()` is not trustworthy evidence** — Electron tracks its own flag and can
   report `true` after the native level dropped. That's why verification is visual
   (fly over a frontmost window), not a logged boolean.
